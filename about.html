My Story
2017 — 2022 · The Foundation

I started by thinking
about systems.

I studied Civil Engineering at Obafemi Awolowo University — graduating with a Second Class Upper and a CGPA of 4.09/5.0. But what engineering really gave me wasn't a technical specialisation. It gave me a way of thinking.

Engineering is fundamentally about understanding how individual parts relate to a whole. What are the constraints? Where is the load? What happens if this component fails? How does a change here affect everything downstream?

I didn't know it then, but I was learning the same questions that good product designers ask — just applied to concrete and steel instead of users and interfaces.

The shift happened in my final year. I discovered digital product design and immediately recognised the parallels. Complex requirements. Multiple stakeholders. Systems that had to work for people with different needs, different contexts, different levels of technical knowledge. The material was different. The thinking was the same.

2021 — 2022 · The Shift

I stopped asking only
"How does it work?"

I completed Google's UX Design Professional Certificate and joined Future Academy Africa's product design bootcamp. For the first time, I had a framework for the questions I'd always been asking — but now applied to people and products instead of structures and loads.

The realisation was straightforward but important: a technically functional product can still be confusing. A visually polished product can still be frustrating. A feature can solve a business problem while creating an entirely new user problem.

Good product design sits in the gap between all of those tensions. That gap became my professional focus.

2022 — 2024 · What I Built On Top

Two more perspectives that
sharpen the design work.

Frontend development came through a course on Udemy and later a scholarship with TS Academy. I can write HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, and I'm building my React knowledge. More importantly, I now understand what happens after the Figma file is handed over — responsive behaviour, component structure, browser constraints, and the gap between design intent and implementation reality.

Data analytics gave me a third lens. SQL, Excel, and Power BI help me investigate what the numbers are saying about how people actually use a product — not how we assumed they would. I can identify where friction is concentrated, which user journeys are failing, and what the data suggests about what to fix next.

These aren't separate disciplines and I don't present them as equally weighted careers. They are perspectives that make my product design work more grounded, more implementation-aware, and more evidence-based.

2022 — Present · The Work

5+ years in.
Still asking better questions.

Since 2022 I've worked as a product designer at Creatifff, Ajiroba Technologies Limited, and Morgen Green Limited. The products have spanned e-commerce, fintech, SaaS, and EdTech. The challenges have been different in every role. But the questions have stayed consistent.

Who is this for? What are they trying to do? Where is the friction? What does the structure need to look like before we design a single screen? How do we make the complexity manageable?

I currently work at Morgen Green Limited on SaaS and EdTech products where workflows are interconnected and the product supports multiple user types with different goals and different levels of technical knowledge. It is exactly the kind of problem I find most interesting.

I'm open to new full-time product design opportunities — remote, hybrid, or relocation.
